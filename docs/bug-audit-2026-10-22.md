# Playback bug audit — 22 October 2026

This pass covers playback intent during connection preparation, scheduled-session
retirement, and HLS sequence parsing, followed by the requested interactive panel
swiping update. Stream endpoints are unchanged.

## Findings and fixes

- **Play ignored during a buffer-mode change.** Turning buffering Off while paused
  starts asynchronous stream preparation before a new player exists. Play returned
  early in that interval, leaving the replacement paused indefinitely. The engine
  now records Play during preparation; a later Pause or Stop still wins.
- **Schedule expiry discarded an active session's safeguards during a stall.**
  At ten minutes after the scheduled time, the request can be retired while its
  audio is temporarily stalled or interrupted. Retirement now follows playback
  intent rather than instantaneous audio progress, preserving the battery guard,
  speaker session and unfinished fade. Explicit Play releases that old automatic
  policy as before.
- **A late fade could jump to full volume after expiry.** A stall before or after
  retirement could either discard the envelope or consume its remaining time
  while no audio played. An unfinished retired fade now waits for readiness and
  restarts its audible minute after a stall. The expired schedule remains removed.
- **An extreme HLS media sequence could trap on integer overflow.** The parser
  now uses checked addition, supports the last representable segment number and
  rejects overflow, malformed, negative, repeated or misplaced sequence headers
  through the normal stream-error path.

## Interactive panel update

switch between Now Playing and Programme. The current panel follows the finger
over the other panel; a short release settles back, while a deliberate swipe or
flick finishes the transition. The Now Playing header is text without a chevron.
Dark backgrounds remain pure black. Vertical scrolling and pull-to-refresh lock
out panel movement, and transport/rewind controls remain outside the gesture area.

reversal and landscape. A new native UI test checks both directions from both
panels and short-drag cancellation; the existing refresh and slider tests remain.
